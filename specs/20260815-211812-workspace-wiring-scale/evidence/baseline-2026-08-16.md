# Baseline sweeps, 2026-08-16

Produced by `task test:scale` (`test/integration/scale/sweep_test.go`) against a
real kcp v0.32.3, with a multicluster manager engaged on every workspace.

The runs themselves are committed alongside this file, and are the record:

- `baseline-active-heavy-19watch.json`
- `baseline-active-heavy-1watch.json`
- `baseline-idle-heavy-1watch.json`

This file states what they mean. Every figure below is re-derivable from them,
which is the point of committing them — a capacity number nobody can recompute
is an assertion, not evidence.

**Load mode: synthetic**, with the caveat FR-039 attaches to that — see
"Known limits".

## The headline

**Event delivery latency does not grow with fleet size**, even at 1,216
listeners on one informer. Memory and goroutines grow **linearly** with no
curvature. No departure from linear was found in any run.

The suspected super-linear dispatch cost did not appear. What binds instead is
the linear-but-large per-workspace footprint.

## Realistic listener count: 19 watches per workspace

The wired Cluster API set registers roughly nineteen watches across five
controllers. Dispatch cost is per listener, so raising watches per workspace
multiplies fan-out without needing more workspaces: 64 workspaces at 19 watches
is the listener count of 1,216 workspaces at one — and concentrated on a
*single* informer, which is harsher than reality spreads it.

| Workspaces | Heap | Goroutines | Deliver p50 | Deliver p99 | Missed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | 15.5 MiB | 1,740 | 6.08 ms | 6.25 ms | 0 |
| 16 | 27.2 MiB | 3,428 | 5.64 ms | 5.97 ms | 0 |
| 32 | 50.5 MiB | 6,804 | 6.81 ms | 8.40 ms | 0 |
| 64 | 96.9 MiB | 13,556 | 6.04 ms | 8.12 ms | 0 |

Marginal: **~1.49 MiB heap, 211 goroutines** per workspace. Goroutine deltas are
211 at every step — 1688/8, 3376/16, 6752/32 — with no drift at all.

## Reproducibility

These are the second run of each configuration. Goroutine counts came back
**identical** at every point, and heap deltas agreed to under one percent. That
matters more than it sounds: a coefficient that moves between runs cannot be
used for sizing, and these do not.

Latency did move — the 19-watch p50 landed between 5.64 and 6.81 ms across the
two runs — which is the natural read of a figure dominated by a network round
trip. It moved without trending.

## What this means for the gate

**Toward closing FR-001** (per-event delivery cost independent of workspace
count). Delivery latency was flat across an 8× fleet increase at both listener
densities, in both runs. The per-listener filter is a closure, a comparison and
a channel send; against a ~6 ms baseline dominated by the round trip to kcp, the
fan-out is not detectable at these scales.

**Toward *building* the footprint requirements.** At 19 watches, 211 goroutines
and ~1.49 MiB per workspace extrapolate to roughly 211,000 goroutines and 1.5 GiB
at 1,000 workspaces. That is the binding constraint, and it is exactly what
FR-003 (bounded per-workspace watch cost) and FR-009/FR-011 (idle cost) address.

The determinations remain unmade — these are inputs to them, not the verdicts.

## Known limits

1. **The p99 is computed from very few samples.** A profile at one event per
   workspace produces 8 latencies at the smallest point and 64 at the largest,
   so "p99" there is the slowest of a handful. It is reported because the tail
   is where a fan-out cost would appear first, but a single elevated value in it
   — the 8.40 ms at 32 workspaces — is one slow round trip, not a trend. Reading
   a trend out of it would need many more events per point.
2. **64 workspaces, not the 256 the fixture reaches** (R10). Anything quoted
   above 64 is an extrapolation and must be labelled one.
3. **A stub reconciler.** The per-workspace cost of registering watches and
   starting workers is what is measured; a production reconciler adds its own
   work on top.
4. **Synthetic load.** Two defects found only against a real server are recorded
   in the commit history: a non-idempotent populate, and a `Touch` that wrote a
   non-monotonic value and so generated no event at all. The second was
   invisible until delivery was instrumented, and showed up as exactly half of
   all events going missing.
