# Baseline sweeps, 2026-08-16

Produced by `task test:scale` (`test/integration/scale/sweep_test.go`) against a
real kcp v0.32.3, with a multicluster manager engaged on every workspace.

The runs themselves are committed alongside this file, and are the record:

- `baseline-idle-heavy-19watch.json` — the profile and density that bound a shard
- `baseline-active-heavy-19watch.json`
- `baseline-idle-heavy-1watch.json`
- `baseline-active-heavy-1watch.json`

`model-accuracy.md` is the fitted reading of them, regenerated by
`task scale:model`. `capacity.md` turns those coefficients into candidate
capacity. This file says what was found.

**Load mode: synthetic**, with the caveat FR-039 attaches to that — see
"Known limits".

## The headline

**Event delivery latency does not grow with fleet size**, even at 1,216
listeners on one informer. Memory and goroutines grow **linearly** with no
curvature. No departure from linear was found in any run.

The suspected super-linear dispatch cost did not appear. What binds instead is
the linear-but-large per-workspace footprint — and it is driven by **listener
count, not by workload**.

## At 19 watches per workspace

The wired Cluster API set registers roughly nineteen watches across five
controllers. Dispatch cost is per listener, so raising watches per workspace
multiplies fan-out without needing more workspaces: 64 workspaces at 19 watches
is the listener count of 1,216 workspaces at one — and concentrated on a
*single* informer, which is harsher than reality spreads it.

## Three things worth stating plainly

**Live heap is not a sizing figure.** It excludes goroutine stacks entirely, and
stacks grow with the fleet — 733 KiB per workspace at 19 listeners, against
1.47 MiB of heap. The process footprint (`MemStats.Sys`) is 1.9× to 2.4× the
live-heap figure, and it is what a container limit should come from. Sizing from
heap alone would under-provision by that multiple.

**An idle workspace costs nearly as much as a busy one.** 2.72 against 3.54 MiB,
and *identical* goroutine counts. Listener registration is the cost; ten objects
and one event per second add about 800 KiB on top of it. FR-026 is right that
the profiles differ, but they differ by 1.3×, not by an order of magnitude.

**Delivery latency is flat.** p50 stays within 5.5–6.0 ms across an 8× fleet
increase at 19 listeners, with nothing missed, and moves without trending.
Against a baseline dominated by the round trip to kcp, a per-listener filter —
a closure, a comparison, a channel send — is not detectable at these scales.

## A measurement defect that held-out validation caught

The first run at 19 listeners fitted badly: R²=0.957 on live heap, and holding
out the smallest point mispredicted it by **54%**. The 1-watch runs from the same
build fitted at R²=1.0000. The defect was in the harness, not the system.

`sample()` ran one `runtime.GC()` before reading `MemStats`. One cycle queues
finalizers but does not run them, so objects awaiting one are still reachable
and get reported as live. How much that inflates the reading depends on what
happened to be in flight — which at 19 listeners and 64 workspaces is a great
deal. Sampling after two cycles fixed it: two consecutive repeats then agreed to
**0.1%** at every point, and the fit returned to R²=1.0000.

Worth recording for two reasons. The bad run's numbers were not obviously wrong
— 78 MiB where 98 MiB belonged is not an implausible figure, and nothing but the
held-out error flagged it. And a 20% error in the direction of *under*-reporting
memory is exactly the error that produces an under-provisioned limit.

## What this means for the gate

**Toward closing FR-001** (per-event delivery cost independent of workspace
count). Delivery latency was flat across an 8× fleet increase at both listener
densities, across repeated runs.

**Toward *building* the footprint requirements.** 211 goroutines and 2.72 MiB
per idle workspace extrapolate to ~169,000 goroutines and ~2.2 GiB at 800
workspaces. That is the binding constraint, and it is exactly what FR-003
(bounded per-workspace watch cost) and FR-009/FR-011 (idle cost) address — with
the idle figures now carrying particular weight, since an idle workspace turns
out to cost nearly what a busy one does.

The determinations remain unmade — these are inputs to them, not the verdicts.

## Known limits

1. **The p99 rests on very few samples.** A profile at one event per workspace
   produces 8 latencies at the smallest point and 64 at the largest, so "p99"
   there is the slowest of a handful. It is reported because the tail is where a
   fan-out cost would appear first, but single elevated values in it are one slow
   round trip, not a trend. Reading a trend out of it needs many more events per
   point.
2. **64 workspaces, not the 256 the fixture reaches** (R10). Anything quoted
   above 64 is an extrapolation and must be labelled one.
3. **CPU is not measured at all**, so no CPU sizing figure can be derived.
4. **A stub reconciler.** The per-workspace cost of registering watches and
   starting workers is what is measured; a production reconciler adds its own
   work on top.
5. **Synthetic load.** Three defects found only against a real server are
   recorded in the commit history: a non-idempotent populate, a `Touch` that
   wrote a non-monotonic value and so generated no event at all, and the
   single-GC sampling above. The second was invisible until delivery was
   instrumented; the third was invisible until the fit was validated.
